Falsify

An autonomous research lab, built on Omnigent, that studies when AI agents stop correcting themselves, and audits its own work while doing it.
Hack-Nation 7th Global AI Hackathon · Challenge 3: Agentic Scientific Discovery (Databricks × Omnigent) · solo · github.com/daylorwilliams5/falsify

Challenge & user

End-to-end agentic research: literature → hypothesis → experiment → analysis → adversarial challenge → next decision. User: AI-safety and agent-systems researchers who need causal evidence about why agent behaviour changes, not more pass/fail benchmarks.

What we built

  • Omnigent research organization: a PI agent, four pods (literature, design, analysis, adversarial; 8 sub-agents), and an independent methodology reviewer that audits every decision.
  • Authority enforced in code, not prompts:
    • L1: the PI acts alone.
    • L2: needs a reviewer PASS.
    • L3: needs a human approval card in Omnigent.
    • Specs are hash-locked; unknown or missing settings fail closed.
  • Controlled subject environments (FreightRoute) with immutable trial logs, preregistrations, an append-only timeline, and a hard $20 API spend cap.

Tools

Omnigent 0.16 (orchestration, policies, approval gates) · Claude (lab agents) · subjects: qwen3:8b via Ollama and Claude Haiku 4.5 via the Anthropic API (structured outputs) · Python (environments, analysis, Clopper-Pearson and bootstrap CIs) · 129 tests.

What worked

  • Executable oversight. The adversarial pod ran a script that never reads the environment. It passed the preregistered validity bar while acting unsafely in 20/20 trials, so the lab threw out a bar everyone (including the human) had approved.
  • The reviewer runs code, not summaries. It blocked a run over a silent $8,000 default in the engineer's build.
  • Honest nulls. Each null was reported with an upper bound and an explicit claim boundary.
  • The design pod argued the follow-up down from three drafts before spending a cent.

What was hard

  • Instrument validity: a livelocked environment, then an inverted validity bar.
  • A small subject model that misread numbers in both directions.
  • Omnigent's one-shot mode silently stopping sessions.
  • Review spirals, fixed with a materiality policy.
  • Two harness failures caught before any money was spent.

Results (Claude Haiku 4.5)

1 · Under ambiguity, it checks (exp012, 90 trials, preregistered).

Evidence that the current plan was invalid was clear, probabilistic, or conflicting; agents worked alone or in teams of four peers.

  • Persisting on the invalid plan: 0/90.
  • With ambiguous evidence, agents inspected first in 60/60. The 95% upper bound on persistence is 4.9%.
  • Peer pressure was untested: the peers never disagreed, which the lab flagged rather than claimed.

2 · Advice, not cost, erodes checking (exp013t, 60 trials).

Inspected before committingColleagues silentColleagues: "skip it"
Inspection free (1 round)15/1511/15
Inspection costly (2 of 6 rounds)15/1510/15
  • Advisory majority: −30 pts of verification (95% CI −13 to −47).
  • Time cost: no detectable effect (+3 pts, CI −13 to +20).
  • Every agent that skipped the check deferred to the safe route its colleagues recommended. Unsafe actions: 0/60.
  • All manipulation and validity checks passed.
Social discouragement erodes information-seeking. In this setting it never tipped into unsafe action, because the advice happened to be safe. Caveats: n = 15 per cell, and the advice text names "skipping".

How the time was spent

In one day (first logged run 10:41, results frozen by 22:00):

  • 6 experiments and 21 PI decisions, each independently reviewed, every one in timeline.jsonl.
  • Two instrument failures and one inverted validity bar caught.
  • One model pivot, made on evidence.
  • About $6 of total subject-model API spend.
